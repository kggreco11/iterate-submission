Results so far

This is the detailed quantitative companion to the research report in RESEARCH_REPORT.md. The report guide distinguishes current interpretation from historical studies.

What the evidence supports

Sharing spreads usable designs quickly, but can concentrate a swarm on one family of plants. Independent search preserves more alternatives, yet those alternatives have not produced the best observed endpoint in either swarm comparison. The current evidence supports a quality–diversity tradeoff, not a general claim that more agents or more memory improve search efficiency.

The run inventory is the current count of uniquely identified experiments; agent members and duplicate exports are not independent repeats. Completed, round-limit and user-stopped outcomes remain separate.

The strongest comparisons

On V3 dev_000, the completed shared swarm without Past reached reward 2.6249 versus 2.4383 for the solo run: a 7.65% endpoint gain. It consumed 1,978 charged queries versus 395, and approximately $19.81 versus $6.83 in recorded model spend. The extra $154.9M/year of modeled plant net value is an optimization objective under benchmark assumptions, not realized revenue or a return on the API bill.

At the same 395-query checkpoint, that swarm had reward 2.1029, 13.76% below the solo result. The earlier behavior report’s 2.1971 figure belongs to the 400-query checkpoint and should not be labeled a 395-query comparison. The master chart uses the exact observed cutoff, with no interpolation or extension beyond a run’s observed endpoint.

The independent five-agent control was stopped at 1,906 of 2,000 allocated queries. Its reward was 2.2395, with approximately $25.93 of saved model usage and five distinct final topologies. It was 14.69% below the shared swarm’s final score and 8.16% below the solo endpoint, but the swarm endpoints use different consumed budgets. At 395 total queries, independent search reached 1.9360. Its backend-call count is unknown after forced shutdown; charged queries are still available from the trajectory.

On V3 dev_002, the separate ten-agent, 500-total-query screen reached 2.5520 with broadcast sharing, 2.4357 with islands, and 2.4276 with independent search. Broadcast was only about 5.1% above independent search, while converting all ten agents to positive plants rather than four of ten. Islands kept five final structures, independent search four, and broadcast one. Islands were 4.56% below broadcast. These are single realizations, not estimates of a reliable policy ranking.

A crashed solo control on dev_002 had already reached 2.6558 after 256 queries, higher than any completed screening team. Its partial status does not invalidate that observed plant, but it excludes the run from completed-run counts. This is an important counterexample to a blanket “teams beat solo” narrative.

Are agents copying, and is search greedy?

The five-agent shared runs selected another agent’s design as their source in roughly 68% of submissions. They selected the incumbent as source in roughly 76–79%. Every final agent-best plant within each shared run had the same topology, although operating settings still differed. This is evidence of strong design inheritance and structural convergence, not identical actions or proof that all agents contribute nothing. Each agent contributed improvements somewhere in the trajectory.

The ten-agent broadcast screen independently shows whole-plant copy operations in 52.2% of recorded turns and one final separation structure. Islands used about 14.3% copy operations and retained five structures. Lessons-only sharing had no explicit plant copies, yet scored only 0.7151. Preventing copying alone is therefore not enough: the shared information must still help agents assemble profitable plants.

Best-so-far retention is greedy by definition, but proposals were not restricted to improvements. The logs include invalid experiments, worse candidates, topology changes, and larger edits. Similar best-so-far curves can conceal different proposals; they do not establish that the underlying policy is a greedy optimizer. Separate incumbent retention, source selection, and proposal novelty in any narrative.

“Radical” also needs a defined metric. A root edit can affect an entire downstream plant, while several parameter edits may remain local. The earlier behavioral audit found more multi-node and root changes in the Past condition, despite its worse endpoint. Measure structural distance, edited positions, inherited settings, newly evaluated columns, and discovery of new profitable plant families rather than using edit count as a single creativity score.

Memory and architecture

Past performed worse in the 400-per-agent campaign: solo reward 1.3800 versus 2.4383 without Past; swarm 2.4553 versus 2.6249. The same directional claim does not hold at the 100-per-agent solo pilot, where Past scored 0.6060 versus 0.4255. These stochastic, single-run outcomes and changing harness versions do not identify a causal memory effect.

The integration also gave retrieved memories weak exposure: about 78% of candidate memory instances were omitted from solo prompts and 86% from swarm prompts. These are repeated admission opportunities, not fractions of unique memories that were never seen. Mandatory incumbent/state and recent exact results took priority. Pending ingestion further limited timely availability. The experiment tested the deployed pipeline, not an ideal memory service. Past is retained as historical evidence; no more Past work is proposed here.

The shared exact-result channel is already a powerful form of memory. A corpus of repeated best plants is not the same as accumulated unique learning. Useful diagnostics are unique source records, deduplicated hypotheses, failure explanations, actual prompt admission, evidence age, and which evidence changed a proposal. Count publication, retrieval, admission, citation, and behavioral adoption separately.

Round barriers make evidence timing easy to audit, but synchronous sharing of the leader can synchronize search. The previous architecture also favored the most recent source records and exposed agents unevenly. Changing round frequency is not needed to investigate this: keep cadence fixed while altering what information is admitted and preserving an agent’s own experimental history. The completed corpus campaign underperformed with immediate sharing; the delayed conditions were cancelled, so it cannot resolve the effect of delaying visibility. See the diagnostic appendix for the observed selection limitation and causal uncertainty. Model, swarm size, prompt contents, and visibility gates changed in various plans; a configuration manifest is necessary to know what actually ran.

Economics and product grades

The best nominal five-agent no-Past plant is economically fragile under a stricter grading rule. Raising the qualification threshold by just 0.05 percentage points reduces its fixed-flow modeled net value by 29.62%, compared with 20.01% for the Past swarm, 3.73% for the stopped independent control, and zero at that increment for the two solo plants. Three no-Past swarm blend products sit around 95.003%, 95.009%, and 95.027% purity.

This is a deterministic regrading sensitivity: flows and plant costs are held fixed, and products are repriced under stricter thresholds. It is not a probability of failure, a process perturbation simulation, or an estimate of expected losses. The independent plant’s better headroom does not establish greater physical robustness.

The most useful product charts pair grade headroom in percentage points with annual product revenue exposed to the threshold, then show net value across explicit threshold shifts. Add recovery by component family to reveal unmonetized material. Profit decomposition matters: the Past swarm earned about $29.6M/year more revenue but incurred about $170.4M/year more annual cost, leaving net value $140.8M/year lower. Premium output alone is not success.

A future physical robustness study would perturb feed, pressure, operating controls, and solver conditions, then re-evaluate material balance, qualification, and net value. That is an unperformed experiment, not something this accounting plot proves.

Historical experiments and task changes

HC12: three completed GPT pilots establish runtime and search baselines. Luna at 50 queries scored 0.7750; Sol at 50 scored 3.3784; a separate Sol 200-query run scored 3.1705. The longer run did not outperform the shorter run, illustrating seed/path variability. Four Opus first looks ended on API errors; their partial best plants and recorded lower-bound spend are retained separately.

V2: four Opus first looks across dev_000–003 scored 3.6292, 4.6312, 7.3802, and 5.1934. On dev_000, Kimi solo scored 3.4219 at 200 queries versus 3.3566 for the Past swarm at 454 consumed queries. The swarm’s historical feedback/context defects, early dollar stops, and different budgets prevent a clean architecture conclusion. These defects were investigated before the V3 protocol; do not transfer the same diagnosis automatically to V3.

V3 first looks: Opus exceeded Kimi on each of four development tasks. On dev_000 through dev_003, Opus scored 2.7669, 2.8531, 5.1323, and 4.4425; Kimi scored 0.5220, 0.2695, 2.1325, and 0.9099. Opus had 400-query ceilings and consumed 110–284; Kimi had 200-query ceilings and consumed 190–200. This is consistent across these observed tasks, but mixes model, effort, stopping behavior, and potentially harness details. Later Kimi dev_000 results improved substantially under another setup, so “Kimi cannot solve V3” is unsupported.

V3 100-query pilot: the three completed conditions were solo exact (0.4255), solo Past (0.6060), and shared exact swarm (2.0698 at 488 team queries). This precedes the 400-per-agent study and should not be pooled as a repeat. The incomplete fourth condition is a coverage gap.

Task versions change the economics and feasible design space. V2 introduces 16 components, blends, premium pricing, and cost multipliers; V3 adds side draws and double-effect options and raises the energy multiplier. Reward is normalized against a task-specific stream value. Do not rank HC12, V2, and V3 rewards on one global leaderboard. Even dev_000 versus dev_002 changes the task instance.

Simulator qualification, robot baselines, repricing, and local polish are separate evidence classes. The historical reference inventory has 81 rows; they are not 81 model runs. Local polish gives evidence of remaining parameter headroom but is not another model trial. Qualification documents nonconvergence and retry behavior; the dev_002 screen enabled alternative initializations while the dev_000 400-query study recorded retries disabled.

What remains unresolved

We have too few repeats to distinguish small condition differences from search variability. Existing experiments answer where these particular searches went, not which policy has the highest expected value. Budget matching must consider both team-total queries and per-agent depth. Charged queries, backend calls, cache reuse, and wall time measure different resources and must remain separate.

The latest Kimi corpus campaign has ended: solo completed at reward 2.3238 and 395 queries; immediate sharing completed at 0.52089 and 1,990 queries. Delay25 was briefly started and user-stopped; delay50 never started. See corpus-comparison.html for the completed comparison and diagnostic limits. Earlier DeepSeek dev_000 attempts encountered policy/adapter failures. One failure is artifact-verified at one charged query with a missing tool response; the second attempt lacks a final summary in the inspected files. The final dev002 study is reconciled above. The F3-100 follow-up still lacks an independently reconciled run summary.

The completed campaign outputs are integrated. The unresolved question is how much the observed evidence-selection limitation contributed to the weak search path; these single runs cannot establish that counterfactual. Any future controlled comparison would need to separate corpus quality from source-design visibility and hold sharing cadence fixed. No further campaign runs or retries are authorized.

Measurement and evidence rules

Completed means the recorded run finished under its stopping rules, not that it consumed its entire allocation. An empty cost is unknown, never zero. Recorded model spend excludes infrastructure and external memory costs and is not an invoice; partial/error runs can have unpriced requests. The inventory deduplicates run IDs across canonical paths, legacy directories, and branches. It does not sum per-agent rows as independent experiments.

All charts use saved artifacts. The master registry links each run to evidence, records fetched commit IDs and source hashes, and lists unclassified or excluded summaries in coverage.json. Existing detailed reports remain the source for prompt-level audits and product-level tables. The master is an aggregate entry point; original results have not been moved or deleted.

## Corpus campaign diagnostic appendix (completed)

The new Kimi immediate-sharing swarm completed at reward 0.52089 after 1,990 total charged queries ($24.24 recorded model spend), versus solo 2.32380 after 395 queries ($4.73). At 395 total queries the swarm had reached 0.393899. The task matches and objective/material accounting checks pass. The swarm winner is a four-column-position plant with much less revenue; all five retained best plants share one topology.

Peer sources were selected in 484/763 submissions. The corpus grew to 565 keys/727 observations, but 7,427/15,025 candidate-source instances entered prompts; admitted non-incumbent sources had median age 66 rounds. The frozen selector rotates insertion-ordered groups/observations, chooses first support IDs, and caps candidates at 20. It does not rank freshness, value improvement or relevance. This documents an evidence-selection limitation, not its causal impact on reward.

This remains a diagnostic appendix rather than a headline conclusion for or against shared-memory architecture. See full analysis, portable admission evidence, and source hashes. Delay25 was briefly started before user cancellation reached the VM: 15 saved submissions, at least 20 logged charged queries, no observed sharing activation. Delay50 never started. No delayed-sharing efficacy conclusion is possible.
