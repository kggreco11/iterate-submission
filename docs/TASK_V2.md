# Task V2: Hydrocarbon-16 (`hc16v2`)

A separate task family. Hydrocarbon-12 (`hc12`) is unchanged and remains the default; pick V2 with `--family hc16v2` (or `$TASK_FAMILY=hc16v2`).
The column settings and the design interface are the same (four settings per column, a tree of columns); what changes is the problem and the economics.

## What changed from Hydrocarbon-12

| | Hydrocarbon-12 | Hydrocarbon-16 V2 |
|---|---|---|
| Components | 12 (ethane to n-octane) | **16**: adds propylene, 3-methylpentane, cyclohexane, n-nonane |
| Orderings of sharp splits | 58,786 | **9,694,845** |
| Close-boiling pairs | iC4/nC4 11 K, iC5/nC5 8 K, 2-MP/nC6 8 K, nC6/MCP 3 K | adds propylene/propane **5.6 K**, 2-MP/3-MP **3.0 K**, 3-MP/nC6 5.5 K, MCP/cyclohexane 9 K |
| Product grades | 95% pure sells at the price | 95% sells at the price; **99% sells at 1.2x** (premium grade) |
| Blends | none | five blends sell without separating their members (below) |
| Plant cost | calibrated cost model | **energy price x2, capital cost x10** on the same cost model |
| Feed pressure | 17.6 bar | **10 to 25 bar by task** (feed temperature 400 K) |
| Column cap | 36 | 48 (3 x components, as before; not an arbitrary lower cap) |

## Economics (stated to the agent in its brief)
- **Pure product:** a stream with at least 95% of one component sells at (flow of that component) x (price) x 8,000 h. At least 99% sells at 1.2x.
- **Blends:** a stream whose members together make up at least 95% of its moles sells as the blend at **85% of the members' mean price**, without
  separating them: C3 mix (propylene + propane), C4 mix (isobutane + n-butane), C5 mix (isopentane + n-pentane), C6 paraffin mix (2,2-dimethylbutane +
  2-methylpentane + 3-methylpentane + n-hexane), C6 naphthene mix (methylcyclopentane + cyclohexane). A stream that qualifies both as pure and as a blend
  takes the higher value. This creates the decision "separate the close boilers for the full price, or sell the mix at a discount".
- **Reward** = (product value - annual column cost) / (value of the most valuable pure stream in the feed, base grade). The unreachable ceiling is the
  reward if every component were sold pure in the premium grade at zero cost (`env.econ.upper_bound_reward`).
- **Cost level:** the energy part of every column's annual cost is doubled and the capital part multiplied by ten. The cost model's capital share was
  4-5% of plant cost; at x10 it is roughly 30-40%, so column size now matters. This is a scenario ("expensive plant"), not a calibration.
  `make_service(..., task=task)` applies it, and `make_env` refuses a service built with different multipliers.

## Tasks
Generated by `hc16v2_tasks("dev")` (4 tasks, seed 1601) and `hc16v2_tasks("test")` (12 tasks, seed 1602): composition ~ Dirichlet around a fixed base mix,
prices x lognormal (sigma 0.15), feed pressure uniform 10-25 bar. Snapshots of the dev tasks: `tasks/hc16v2_dev_00N.json`.
Hashes: dev `66b06e7b6ff1`, test `f1f68a21ba70` (first 12 characters of the SHA-256 of the sorted JSON). Test tasks are not to be used for tuning.

The ceilings differ more than in V1 because the normaliser (the most valuable single pure stream) varies with each task's mix and prices; compare agents
as a share of each task's best known design, not by raw reward.

## Qualification on real ChemSep (16 components, all four dev pressures)
1,200 column solves in robot runs: 2.0% did not converge (24 of 1,200), median 0.47 s and mean 0.92 s per solve. Environment tests: 35 passed (V2 rules,
premium/blend classification, cost-multiplier refusal, plus the existing validation and runtime tests). **Not done:** a comparison of the 16-component
backend against a second simulator, an independent energy-balance check, or a calibration of the cost model for propylene and n-nonane; the capital x10
and energy x2 multipliers act on a model fitted to six hydrocarbons and extrapolating beyond them.

## Early robot behaviour (tentative: one seed, 150 solves)
Random restarts reached 0.00 to 1.08 and first-cut-plus-greedy 0.42 to 2.84 across the four dev tasks, well below the V1 robots at the same budget. This was the initial qualification snapshot. Later agent runs and polish checks are recorded in [Opus V2 first look](../results/hc16v2-first-look/README.md), and the Kimi pair is documented in the [historical V2 comparison](V2_KIMI_COMPARISON.md). V2 robot baselines at 400 solves remain outstanding.

## Caveats
- The 85% blend price, the 1.2x premium and the x2 / x10 cost multipliers are benchmark assumptions chosen to create decisions, not market data.
- "Net" still ignores the cost of the feed itself, and off-spec streams are still worth zero.
- Results on V2 are not comparable with V1 results.
- More components and blends make "how close to optimal?" harder to answer; use the best known design (agents plus the local polish in
  `scripts/polish_check.py`) as the reference and report scores against it, not against a proven optimum.

## Rollback
Nothing in V1 was changed: `--family hc12` (the default) reproduces the previous tasks, hashes and rules.
